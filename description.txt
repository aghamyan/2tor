TUTORING PLATFORM — FULL PRODUCT, TECHNICAL, PRIVACY, AND DELIVERY SPECIFICATION
Version: 1.0
Status: Product definition for validation, design, and implementation
Prepared from founder requirements
Primary launch market: Armenian-American families in selected U.S. states
Primary delivery model: Managed online school
Initial subjects: Mathematics, Armenian language and heritage, Programming
Interface languages: English and Armenian

======================================================================
0. EXECUTIVE DECISION SUMMARY
======================================================================

This product will be a managed online tutoring school connecting students in the United States with tutors in Armenia. It is not an open marketplace at launch. The company controls tutor verification, student-tutor matching, pricing, quality standards, curriculum expectations, communications, academic reporting, and dispute handling.

The platform’s strongest product promise is not “cheap tutoring.” Cheap tutoring alone is easy to copy and usually produces weak retention. The defensible promise is:

“Structured online tutoring with verified Armenian educators, personalized learning plans, visible progress, and parent accountability.”

The platform will initially focus on Armenian-American families because the founder has a credible cultural and operational advantage in that market. A nationwide, all-subject, all-age launch is rejected as unrealistic for a two-developer team. The platform may technically accept users from many locations, but initial marketing, tutor preparation, curriculum content, customer support, and operational processes should be concentrated.

Recommended initial market:
- California
- Maryland
- One additional test market selected from Oregon, Massachusetts, New York, or New Jersey based on customer interviews and Armenian-family concentration
- English and Armenian interfaces
- Mathematics, Armenian language/heritage, and beginner programming
- Grades 4–9 as the operational focus, while allowing carefully approved exceptions for younger, older, and adult students

The platform will use Zoom for live classes during the MVP. It will not build native video conferencing. The website will manage scheduling, Zoom links, assignments, submissions, whiteboard/resource links, learning plans, tutor feedback, milestones, messaging, billing records, and administration.

The MVP will be a modular monolith, not microservices. It will use:
- Next.js with TypeScript
- PostgreSQL hosted on the company-controlled server
- Docker Compose
- Redis for queues, caching, rate limiting, and ephemeral real-time state
- S3-compatible object storage, preferably AWS S3, for student files and exports
- Nginx or Caddy as reverse proxy
- Background worker process
- Stripe for U.S. card payments
- PayPal only after Stripe flow is stable
- Web Push and email notifications
- Automated off-site backups
- AWS deployment with a documented path from one server to multiple application instances and managed database services

The founder requested a “local database” and a “single server.” That is acceptable only for an early private beta. A single server is a single point of failure. Therefore:
- The application and PostgreSQL may initially run in Docker on one AWS EC2 instance.
- Backups must be encrypted and copied automatically to a separate AWS account or storage location.
- Uploaded files must not live only on the server disk.
- Database access must never be public.
- The architecture must permit later migration to Amazon RDS without rewriting the application.
- Production, staging, and development must remain separate even if the initial production system is small.

1. PRODUCT VISION
======================================================================

1.1 Mission

Help children and adult learners receive high-quality, culturally connected, accountable online education from qualified Armenian tutors, while giving parents a transparent view of what is being taught, what the student has completed, where the student is struggling, and what should happen next.

1.2 Problem

Most private tutoring is fragmented:
- Lessons happen in Zoom.
- Homework is sent through WhatsApp.
- Payments are tracked manually.
- Parents receive vague feedback.
- Learning plans live in the tutor’s head.
- Progress is hard to measure.
- Tutor quality is inconsistent.
- Students lose motivation.
- There is no reliable operational record when a tutor changes.
- Messaging can move outside the company, allowing tutors and clients to bypass the platform.
- Children may communicate privately with adults without adequate oversight.

The platform solves the operational system around tutoring. It should not pretend that software replaces the tutor. Tutors remain responsible for educational judgment. The platform captures evidence, enforces workflow, gives structure, and makes progress visible.

1.3 Product principles

1. Human-led education
The tutor is responsible for curriculum decisions, feedback, milestone evaluation, and adaptation. Software supports the tutor; it does not automatically make high-impact academic decisions in the MVP.

2. Parent visibility
Parents can see schedules, attendance, assignments, submissions, feedback, learning-plan progress, milestones, billing records, and relevant communications.

3. Child safety by design
No private tutor-child direct messaging. Communications are visible to the parent and auditable by authorized administrators. Minimum necessary data is collected.

4. Measurable progress
Each student has a baseline, goals, a learning plan, completed work, tutor feedback, milestone evidence, and periodic progress reviews.

5. Operational control
The company verifies tutors, controls matching, defines teaching standards, reviews performance, manages disputes, and can suspend access.

6. Fast and simple
The platform should feel faster and easier than using several disconnected tools. Core pages must load quickly and work well on weak connections.

7. Honest claims
The company must not guarantee grade improvement or “no cheating.” It can promise qualified tutoring processes, progress tracking, transparent reporting, and reasonable anti-cheating controls.

2. POSITIONING AND BUSINESS MODEL
======================================================================

2.1 Positioning

Primary market:
Armenian-American families seeking high-quality, flexible, online academic support and Armenian cultural education.

Secondary market:
Non-Armenian U.S. families seeking affordable, accountable tutoring after initial validation.

Brand story:
- Armenian tutors are publicly part of the brand.
- Tutors are selected and verified by the school.
- Tutors receive internal U.S.-curriculum training.
- Lessons combine academic rigor, personal attention, cultural understanding, and modern digital workflows.

2.2 Recommended homepage promise

“Personalized online tutoring with verified Armenian educators—and progress parents can actually see.”

Supporting copy:
“Math, programming, and Armenian heritage lessons built around each learner’s goals, with structured feedback, milestones, projects, and support between classes.”

2.3 Revenue model

MVP:
- One-time individual lessons
- Free 30–45 minute consultation or trial
- Postpaid or weekly invoicing only for a tightly controlled private beta
- Recommended public launch model: payment authorization before service or prepaid balance, because pure postpaid tutoring creates collection risk
- Company sets customer price
- Tutor compensation is set separately
- Group lessons priced per student
- Sibling and referral discounts controlled by administrators

Initial example economics:
- Parent price: $18 per standard lesson
- Tutor gross compensation: $12
- Company gross margin before fees and expenses: $6

This pricing is probably too low for a durable U.S.-facing business once card fees, refunds, customer support, software, tutor supervision, free trials, failed payments, taxes, and acquisition costs are included. It must be validated with a financial model before launch. The platform should support variable price rules even if one price is initially used.

Phase 2:
- Monthly lesson packages
- Recurring subscriptions
- Tiered tutor pricing
- Group course packages
- Premium outside-class support
- Subject-specific pricing
- SAT/AP premium pricing

2.4 Tutor retention and anti-bypass strategy

Punishing tutors is not enough. Tutors stay when the platform provides value:
- Reliable student acquisition
- Predictable monthly payouts
- Scheduling and reminder tools
- Curriculum resources
- Homework and assessment workflows
- Parent communication records
- Tutor coaching
- Performance profile and progression
- Reduced administrative burden
- Dispute protection
- Group-course opportunities
- Content library
- Bonuses or better rates for reliability and quality

Contracts may prohibit bypassing, but the business must make staying more attractive than leaving.

5. CORE USER FLOWS
======================================================================

5.1 Lead to enrollment

1. Parent visits subject or heritage-course page.
2. Parent selects “Book a free consultation.”
3. Parent enters minimum details:
   - parent name
   - email
   - phone
   - state/time zone
   - student first name or preferred name
   - student age/grade range
   - subject
   - goals
4. Parent receives privacy notice and consent flow.
5. Admin contacts family.
6. Student may complete a free assessment.
7. Tutor or academic reviewer prepares diagnostic notes.
8. Admin selects tutor options.
9. Parent approves schedule and price.
10. Parent account and student account are activated.
11. Zoom link and first lesson appear in dashboards.
12. Tutor creates an initial learning plan after the first one or two sessions.

The tutor should not be the sole creator of the customer record. That creates duplicates and weak consent evidence. Recommended rule:
- Parent submits or confirms the family record.
- Tutor may enter draft academic details.
- Admin reviews and approves.
- Parent confirms required child and consent information.

5.2 Lesson flow

Before:
- Reminder sent according to preferences.
- Tutor sees student goals, last feedback, unfinished assignments, and upcoming lesson plan.
- Student can upload work.
- Zoom link becomes clearly available.

During:
- Zoom is used for video.
- External or integrated collaborative whiteboard is used.
- Tutor can add lesson notes.
- Live quiz may be launched.
- Student can upload answers.
- Platform may record lesson start/end status but does not record video.

After:
- Tutor marks attendance and completion.
- Tutor submits structured feedback:
  - topics covered
  - student engagement
  - assignment performance
  - observed strengths
  - skills to improve
  - next steps
  - homework
  - milestone updates
  - optional private staff note
- Parent and student receive a summary.
- Billing and tutor ledger entries are generated.
- Admin is alerted if feedback is missing after the defined deadline.

5.3 Assessment flow

- Tutor creates or selects assessment.
- Assessment may be timed.
- Student enters assessment mode.
- The platform records:
  - start/end times
  - answer timestamps
  - focus loss events
  - fullscreen exit events
  - connectivity interruptions
  - copy/paste attempts where technically detectable
  - answer changes
- Student is warned that these signals are collected.
- The platform never claims cheating was proven solely from browser signals.
- Tutor reviews answers and signals.
- Tutor writes the diagnostic report.
- Parent receives the report after consultation.

5.4 Rescheduling and cancellation

Recommended default:
- More than 8 hours before lesson: no charge; reschedule permitted.
- Less than 8 hours: 50% charge for individual lessons.
- Student no-show: treated as late cancellation.
- Group lesson absence: full charge.
- Tutor cancellation: no student charge; replacement lesson or credit.
- Tutor late arrival: recorded; repeated lateness affects quality review.
- All exceptions require an administrator reason.

Daylight saving time must be handled by IANA time zones in software, not manually by an administrator.

5.5 Tutor payout

- Every completed or chargeable lesson generates a tutor earning entry.
- Trial lesson generates no tutor payout under current policy; this may hurt tutor recruitment and should be reconsidered.
- Monthly payout report is generated in AMD.
- Exchange rate source and conversion date are recorded.
- Finance reviews and pays to bank account.
- Payment confirmation is attached.
- Tutor can see completed lesson count and expected monthly total, but not sensitive company margin data unless intentionally shown.

7. ENGAGEMENT AND “NO MORE BORING LESSONS”
======================================================================

The phrase must be converted into concrete teaching practices:

MVP:
- Student interest profile
- Tutor selects examples connected to interests
- Visible goals and progress
- Short interactive checks
- Real projects
- Choice between assignment options
- Points for productive actions
- Levels and badges
- Streaks with grace periods
- Seasonal challenges
- Celebration of milestones
- Age-appropriate dashboard design

Rules:
- Points have no monetary value.
- No global public leaderboard for children.
- Competition is opt-in and can be disabled by parents.
- Reward effort, consistency, improvement, and project completion—not only high scores.
- Do not punish a child for missing a streak because of illness or travel.
- Avoid manipulative infinite engagement patterns.

Example point events:
- Complete assignment on time
- Improve from prior attempt
- Ask a thoughtful question
- Complete a milestone
- Help another student with an approved answer
- Finish a project
- Maintain attendance

A student answer in the discussion area may be praised or marked helpful by a tutor. It must not be presented as authoritative until reviewed.

8. GROUP LESSONS AND DISCUSSION
======================================================================

Recommended group size:
- Minimum: 2
- Ideal: 3–5
- Maximum for academic tutoring: 6
- Maximum for discussion-based heritage reading: 8, only with a trained tutor

Group creation criteria:
- age/grade compatibility
- skill compatibility
- shared goal
- schedule compatibility
- language level
- parent consent
- tutor capacity

Group features:
- fixed course dates
- recurring schedule
- roster
- attendance
- announcements
- resources
- assignments
- discussion threads
- tutor moderation
- reports
- mute/remove controls
- incident reporting

Students should not have unrestricted direct messages. Group conversations are retained, reportable, and visible to the tutor and authorized staff. Parent visibility should be age-sensitive, but for minors the parent must be able to access their child’s communication history.

======================================================================
9. STACK OVERFLOW–STYLE KNOWLEDGE AREA
======================================================================

Working name: Learning Questions

Purpose:
- Students ask academic questions outside class.
- Tutors and approved students answer.
- Tutors mark correct or helpful responses.
- Useful answers become searchable knowledge.
- Questions are scoped to a course, group, or subject.

MVP rules:
- No anonymous posting.
- No public indexing of child content.
- No private messaging from a question.
- Student display names use first name plus controlled identifier, not full legal name.
- Tutors moderate.
- Admin can review reports.
- Images and files are scanned.
- Personally identifying information is automatically discouraged and can be flagged.
- Answer voting is limited; no popularity competition.
- A tutor can mark “verified answer.”
- Questions can be private to the student, parent, tutor, and admin.
- Course questions can be shared with the group.
- Response target for the assigned tutor: within 12 hours during agreed service days, not literally 24/7.

Unlimited messaging is financially dangerous. MVP should measure message volume and tutor time. After validation, outside-class support should have fair-use limits or a premium tier.

======================================================================
10. ANTI-CHEATING DESIGN
======================================================================

Browser-based anti-cheating can deter and document suspicious behavior, but it cannot guarantee that a student did not use another phone, another person, notes outside the camera, or a second computer.

MVP controls:
- Clear academic-integrity policy
- Randomized question order
- Randomized values where possible
- Limited copy/paste
- Fullscreen assessment mode
- Visibility API focus-loss tracking
- Fullscreen-exit tracking
- Time-per-question logging
- Answer-change history
- Question pools
- Optional camera requirement for selected assessments, only with explicit consent and policy
- Tutor review of suspicious patterns
- Honor statement before submission
- Oral follow-up for high-stakes assessments
- Similarity detection for text/code submissions
- Programming tasks with individualized inputs and brief explanation requirement

Never:
- Automatically accuse a child
- Treat tab switching as proof
- secretly record camera or screen
- install invasive monitoring software for ordinary tutoring
- use facial recognition or emotion detection
- make “no cheating” a marketing claim

11. PRIVACY, CHILD SAFETY, AND LEGAL REQUIREMENTS
======================================================================

This section is product guidance, not legal advice. A U.S. child-privacy attorney must review the final product before public launch.

11.1 Critical correction

For users under 13, checking a Terms box is not automatically sufficient as verifiable parental consent. The platform must provide direct notice and use a legally acceptable parental consent method before collecting covered child personal information.

Recommended flow:
1. Parent creates the family account.
2. Parent verifies email and phone.
3. Platform provides direct child-data notice.
4. Parent completes verifiable consent using an approved method selected with counsel.
5. Consent record stores version, timestamp, method, data categories, and identity evidence reference.
6. Only then is the child account activated for covered features.

11.2 Data minimization

Collect:
- parent name
- parent email
- parent phone
- parent billing information through payment provider
- student preferred name
- exact birth date only if legally and operationally justified; otherwise store month/year or age band
- grade
- state/time zone
- subject
- goals
- interests
- academic work
- feedback
- attendance
- communications
- required consent records

Do not collect by default:
- home address
- Social Security number
- immigration status
- precise location
- school login credentials
- unnecessary health diagnoses
- biometric identifiers
- facial recognition templates
- contact lists
- advertising identifiers
- unrelated browsing history

11.3 Audio and video

The founder stated both that lesson recording is not required and that voice/video recordings may be collected. Resolve this as:
- Zoom lessons are not recorded by the platform in the MVP.
- Student-submitted audio/video assignments are permitted.
- Each upload has a purpose, access rule, and retention category.
- Public portfolio sharing requires explicit parent approval.
- No background recording.
- No reuse in marketing without separate written consent.

11.4 AI

Student data must not be retained by AI providers for their own model training.
Contrary to the initial answer, allowing AI providers to retain child submissions is rejected.
Before any AI feature launches:
- Execute data processing agreements.
- Confirm no-training terms.
- Minimize and pseudonymize inputs.
- Separate parent consent where required.
- label AI output.
- require human review for academic decisions.
- provide opt-out where practical.
- log prompts and outputs only when necessary and with strict retention.

11.5 Retention

Recommended default:
- Unverified leads: 90 days
- Failed/incomplete registration: 90 days
- Active account academic data: while active and reasonably needed
- Inactive academic data: 2 years, then delete or anonymize unless parent requests earlier deletion and no legal basis requires retention
- Ordinary messages: 2 years after account inactivity, not 10 years
- Safety/incident records: according to legal counsel and incident policy
- Payment and tax records: legally required period, potentially up to 7–10 years depending on jurisdiction
- Audit logs: 2 years minimum for ordinary operations; longer for security incidents
- Deleted files: removed from active systems promptly and aged out of backups on a documented schedule
- Consent records: retained as long as necessary to demonstrate compliance

A blanket right to keep child data for one year after a parent deletion request is not acceptable without a defined lawful reason. The deletion workflow must distinguish legal records from optional educational content.

11.6 Child safety

Required:
- Mandatory tutor safeguarding training
- Identity verification
- Strongly recommended criminal/background screening before public launch
- No private tutor-child messaging
- Parent-visible communication
- Report button
- Incident workflow
- Emergency escalation policy
- Tutor code of conduct
- Boundaries on personal contact
- Documented suspension process
- Admin access logs
- Regular tutor review
- Parent complaint procedure

Launching a child-focused platform with no background checks is a serious trust and safety weakness. At minimum, obtain professional legal advice on Armenian screening options and communicate exactly what “verified tutor” means. Never imply that verification includes a criminal background check unless it does.

11.7 External communication

The requirements conflict:
- Outside-platform communication is prohibited.
- Tutors may share WhatsApp phone numbers.

Recommended resolution:
- No direct one-to-one WhatsApp between tutor and minor.
- For beta operations, a parent+tutor+official company WhatsApp group may be used only when necessary.
- Academic files, assignments, feedback, and important decisions must be copied into the platform.
- Public launch should make in-platform communication the default.
- Phone numbers should not be exposed to students.
- Company policies must state monitoring and retention clearly.

11.8 Copyright

Do not permit downloading or redistributing copyrighted textbook content or YouTube videos without authorization.
For video resources:
- Store YouTube links or embedded players.
- Record metadata and educational tags.
- Do not download videos to company servers.
- Do not claim ownership.
- Remove links when content disappears or is reported.
- Prefer official, licensed, or tutor-created resources.
- Tutors must confirm they have rights to uploaded material.

12. SECURITY REQUIREMENTS
======================================================================

12.1 Authentication

MVP:
- Email and password for parents and tutors
- Username or controlled email login for students
- Email verification
- Strong password policy
- Argon2id password hashing
- Secure password reset
- Multi-factor authentication required for administrators and finance
- Multi-factor authentication strongly recommended for tutors
- Parent MFA optional at launch
- Session list and logout-all-devices
- New-device alerts for staff
- Shorter session lifetime for administrators
- Secure, HttpOnly, SameSite cookies
- CSRF protection

Phase 2:
- Passkeys
- Google and Apple login for parents
- risk-based login checks

12.2 Authorization

Use server-side role and relationship checks for every protected action.
Do not rely on hidden buttons.
Access examples:
- Tutor can access a student only while assigned.
- Parent can access only linked children.
- Finance cannot read academic messages.
- Admin message access requires permission and logged reason.
- Super-admin cannot erase audit trails.

12.3 Encryption

- TLS 1.2+ for all network traffic
- Database and backup encryption at rest
- S3 bucket encryption
- Field-level encryption for sensitive data such as phone number, exact birth date, and identity-document references where practical
- Keys stored in AWS KMS or a secrets manager, not source code
- Separate secrets by environment
- Regular rotation

12.4 Upload security

- Allowlist MIME types
- Verify file signature, not only extension
- Maximum file size by category
- Malware scanning using ClamAV or managed scanning
- Private object storage
- Signed, expiring download URLs
- Image metadata stripping where appropriate
- No executable uploads
- Quarantine until scan completes
- Virus-scan status stored

Recommended limits:
- Images: 15 MB
- PDF/documents: 25 MB
- Audio: 50 MB
- Video assignment: 250 MB initially
- Code archive: 20 MB with extraction disabled by default

12.5 Audit logging

Log:
- login and failed login
- password/MFA changes
- role changes
- student relationship changes
- tutor assignment changes
- consent changes
- admin impersonation
- message access by staff
- exports
- deletions
- refunds and credits
- lesson record edits
- payout changes
- tutor verification decisions
- security-setting changes

Audit logs are append-only to application users and protected from normal deletion.

12.6 Infrastructure security

- EC2 in a private network design where practical
- Only reverse proxy exposed publicly
- PostgreSQL and Redis not public
- SSH disabled or restricted through AWS Systems Manager
- Host firewall
- Docker containers run as non-root where possible
- Read-only filesystems where practical
- Dependency scanning
- Container image scanning
- rate limiting
- bot protection on public forms
- WAF before broader launch
- DDoS baseline via CloudFront and AWS protections
- daily vulnerability alerts
- annual penetration test before significant scale
- incident response runbook

12.7 Backups and recovery

Private beta:
- PostgreSQL encrypted backup every 6 hours
- daily full backup
- 30-day rolling retention
- weekly long-term backup for 3 months
- encrypted off-server copy
- monthly restore test

Targets:
- Recovery point objective: 6 hours in beta, 1 hour after growth
- Recovery time objective: 8 hours in beta, 2 hours after growth

A backup that has never been restored is not a reliable backup.

25. MVP SUCCESS DEFINITION
======================================================================

The MVP is validated when, during a controlled private beta:

- At least 30 paying students have completed one month.
- At least 20 students remain active through 90 days or the cohort retention rate reaches the agreed threshold.
- Parents regularly use progress and feedback features.
- Tutors submit feedback on time without excessive administrative burden.
- Student-tutor matching works without frequent reassignment.
- The business generates positive contribution margin before founder salary and general overhead.
- No severe unresolved child-safety or privacy failure occurs.
- At least 70% of surveyed parents say they would recommend the service.
- The team can clearly identify which subject and age segment has the strongest demand.
- The team can support operations without relying on uncontrolled WhatsApp records or spreadsheets.

If these conditions are not met, adding AI, native video, mobile apps, or more subjects is a distraction.

27. CONCLUSION
======================================================================

The strongest version of this business is not a giant tutoring marketplace and not an AI education platform. It is a focused, managed Armenian-American online school with strong tutor operations, transparent progress, structured parent communication, useful learning tools, and trustworthy child-safety practices.

The founder should resist feature inflation. The difficult part is not building a calendar, chat, or dashboard. The difficult part is proving:
- parents trust the tutors,
- tutors consistently produce quality,
- progress is visible,
- pricing is sustainable,
- communication remains safe,
- and families stay.

Build the operational backbone first. Add automation only after the human process works.
