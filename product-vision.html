01 / Vision

A school, not a directory.

Build a focused online school where human educators, cultural connection, and a transparent learning system help every family understand what is being learned—and what should happen next.

The company manages tutor selection, matching, quality standards, curriculum expectations, communications, reporting, and disputes. The tutor still makes the educational decisions. The platform gives those decisions structure, continuity, and visible evidence.

Deliberate launch focus

Market

4–9 operational focus, with carefully approved exceptions

Managed online school with manual matching at launch

02 / Mission

Make good teaching accountable.

Help children and adult learners receive high-quality, culturally connected online education from qualified Armenian tutors—while giving parents a clear view of what was taught, what was completed, where support is needed, and what comes next.

The software does not replace the educator. Tutors write the feedback, evaluate milestones, and adapt the plan. The system captures the evidence and keeps the family in the loop.

  • Schedules and attendance
  • Assignments and submissions
  • Feedback and challenges
  • Milestones and next steps

03 / What makes it different

The lesson is only one part of learning.

The fragmented normZoom for lessons. WhatsApp for homework. Spreadsheets for payment. Vague updates for parents. The product connects the work around the lesson without pretending to replace the tutor.

  • 01

    Operational control

    Managed, not marketplace.

    The school selects and verifies tutors, matches each learner, sets standards, reviews quality, and handles disputes. “Verified” means only what the documented process can support.

  • 02

    Cultural connection

    Armenian heritage is core.

    Reading, conversation, history, culture, and family heritage projects are central subjects—not a small checkbox beside a generic tutoring offer.

  • 03

    Human judgment

    Tutors lead the learning.

    Educators choose the curriculum, adapt the plan, review evidence, and write feedback. Technology supports judgment; it does not automate high-impact academic decisions.

  • 04

    Visible evidence

    Progress has a paper trail.

    A baseline leads to goals, a learning plan, completed work, feedback, projects, milestones, and periodic reviews—so progress survives a tutor change and does not live only in memory.

  • 05

    Parent accountability

    Families see the whole picture.

    Parents can follow schedules, attendance, assignments, submissions, feedback, learning-plan progress, billing, and relevant communication from one place.

  • 06

    Safety by design

    No hidden adult–child channel.

    Minor accounts are parent-linked. Private tutor-child messaging is excluded. Relevant communication is parent-visible and auditable, with minimum necessary data collected.

The promise is a trustworthy process, not a guaranteed grade. The school can show qualified tutoring practices, visible progress, and reasonable integrity controls—never claim that outcomes are automatic or cheating is impossible.

04 / Roadmap ahead

Advance by proof, not feature count.

No invented launch dates. Every phase opens only when the human operation, safety foundation, and economics of the phase before it are working.

  1. 00

    Validate first

    Prove the school before the software.

    Interview 20 parents and 10 tutors. Run 5–10 tutoring relationships manually. Validate pricing, tutor verification, consent, cancellation rules, feedback templates, subjects, and age focus with professional legal review.

    Gate: demand + reviewed legal foundation
  2. 01

    Build the MVP

    Create the operational backbone.

    Build secure roles and profiles, verification and matching, scheduling and Zoom links, attendance, learning plans, assignments, submissions, human-written feedback, milestones, parent-visible messaging, Stripe records, audit logs, notifications, and administration.

    Gate: one complete, accountable learning flow
  3. 02

    Harden + private beta

    Test trust under real use.

    Complete accessibility and security reviews, restore a backup, test privacy export and deletion, rehearse incident response, enforce administrator MFA, train tutors on safeguarding, and operate a controlled beta for at least one full month.

    Proposed targets for validation—not current traction or guaranteed results.

  4. 03

    After validation

    Expand what families prove they need.

    Add lesson packages and subscriptions, group-course enrollment, richer staff permissions, parent reviews and tutor introductions, calendar sync, PWA support, mastery levels, a question bank, portfolios, improved progress reports, curriculum certification, and dedicated support workflows.

    Gate: retention + visible progress + sound economics
  5. 04

    Later bets

    Automate only after the model works.

    Consider native apps and video, AI-assisted learning, adaptive assessments, school accounts, public integrations, marketplace-style selection, and an internal collaborative whiteboard only when the human teaching model has already earned trust.

    Gate: proven human model + clear new need

Product discipline

Deliberately not now.

Zoom already handles live video. The first advantage comes from the operational system around the lesson: matching, planning, evidence, feedback, parent communication, safety, and reporting.

  • Native video
  • Advanced AI tutor
  • Open marketplace
  • Native mobile apps
  • Microservices
  • Global child leaderboards

Build the human backbone first. Add automation when it removes proven friction.
